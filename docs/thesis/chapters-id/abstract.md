# ABSTRACT {.unnumbered}

Search agents trained with reinforcement learning (RL) are usually rewarded only for a correct final answer, which does not show whether the agent found or can point to the evidence behind it. This thesis studies a judge-free alternative, VEGR (Verifiable Evidence-Grounded Reward). The agent answers with sentence-level citations, and VEGR checks every citation by rule against HotpotQA's gold supporting facts and sums answer, evidence and search components, with a penalty for invalid actions. A Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct agent with LoRA was trained with a GRPO-style policy gradient on one 48 GB GPU, searching each question's 10 paragraphs with BM25. Sixteen runs covered five configurations on three seeds and one diagnostic run, and were evaluated on 300 holdout questions with seed-aware bootstrap confidence intervals (CI). Training raised exact match (EM) by about 20 points over the untrained model (22.3%), mostly by teaching the action protocol: EM on completed episodes rose only from 39.4% to 43.8–45.3%. No accuracy difference was detected between an answer-only (outcome-only) reward and VEGR (+1.0 EM, 95% CI [−3.6, +5.4]). Under the outcome-only reward, 2 of 3 seeds stopped citing; the training logs are consistent with the explanation that invalid citations are penalised with −1 while correct ones add almost no reward. VEGR raised precision-grounded EM by 24.4 points over outcome-only (CI [+8.8, +37.3]), mainly because those two seeds cited nothing, and this metric uses the same gold labels as the reward. VEGR did not raise evidence coverage over the untrained model. In exploratory runs, a larger coverage weight traded precision for completeness. In this setting, grounding becomes visible only when it is measured explicitly; accuracy alone does not reveal it.

**Keywords**: retrieval-augmented generation, search agents, reinforcement learning, verifiable rewards, citation grounding, multi-hop question answering.
