\chapter*{Abstract}\label{abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Search agents trained with reinforcement learning (RL) are usually
rewarded only for a correct final answer, which does not show whether
the agent found or can point to the evidence behind it. This thesis
studies a judge-free alternative, VEGR (Verifiable Evidence-Grounded
Reward). The agent answers with sentence-level citations, and VEGR
checks every citation by rule against HotpotQA's gold supporting facts.
It rewards correct answers, correct citations and useful searches, and
it penalises invalid actions. A Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct agent with LoRA was
trained with a GRPO-style policy gradient on one 48 GB GPU. The agent
searches a closed pool of each question's 10 paragraphs with BM25.
Sixteen runs (about \$49) covered five configurations on three seeds and
one diagnostic run, and were evaluated on a frozen 300-question holdout
with seed-aware bootstrap confidence intervals. Training raised exact
match (EM) by about 20 points over the un-tuned model (22.3\%), mostly
by teaching the action protocol: EM on completed episodes rose only from
39.4\% to 45.2\%. The answer-only reward and the full VEGR bundle
reached the same accuracy (+1.0 EM, 95\% CI {[}−3.6, +5.4{]}). Under the
answer-only reward, 2 of 3 seeds stopped citing, because invalid
citations cost −1 while correct ones earned nothing. VEGR raised
precision-grounded EM by 24.4 points (CI {[}+8.8, +37.3{]}) but did not
raise evidence coverage over the un-tuned model. A larger coverage
weight traded precision for completeness and scored best on HotpotQA's
Joint-EM. On the one seed tested, dynamic sampling did not prevent the
loss of citations. Grounding has to be rewarded and measured explicitly;
accuracy alone does not reveal it.

\textbf{Keywords:} search agents, reinforcement learning, GRPO,
verifiable rewards, citation grounding, multi-hop question answering,
HotpotQA
